title: "Security Layer: Self vs Non-self Discrimination"
date: Saturday 25 July 2026
venue: London
summary: Self/non-self discrimination as the immune system's security layer — and autoimmunity as what happens when it is breached. With Prof. Mark Coles, the room asked whether immune "permission" works more like a distributed access-control system shaped by tissue context, what infection-gated prophylaxis could look like, and whether AI could rebuild how we classify autoimmune disease from the raw biology up.
speakers:
  - name: Prof. Mark Coles
    role: Professor of Immunology, University of Oxford
    photo: /meetings/speakers/mark-coles.jpg
questions:
  - Could self/non-self discrimination be understood as a biological access-control system — and if so, what does autoimmunity reveal about how that system fails?
  - Can prophylaxis move beyond hard-coded interventions like vaccines toward programmable immune states governed by infection-gated logic circuits — and how do we keep that protection from triggering inflammatory pathologies of its own?
  - Is there a single best computational analogy for what triggers autoimmunity — a buffer overflow, a race condition, a spoofing attack, a denial-of-service against the regulatory layer — or do different diseases genuinely need different metaphors?
  - How many autoimmune diseases are, at root, post-infectious processes we have failed to trace back to their origin — and what technologies could help us trace and debug them?
  - If you deleted every layer above the raw data and let an AI agent rebuild the classification, diagnostic and intervention layers itself, would it reconverge on today's specialties — or build something we would not recognise?
