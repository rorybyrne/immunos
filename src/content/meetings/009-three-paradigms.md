title: "Memory Management: Ex Vivo Therapy versus In Vivo Immune Engineering"
date: Saturday 24 October 2026
venue: Jesus College, Oxford
open: true
summary: How does immunological memory differ when we engineer immunity outside the body versus inside it? Three speakers, three paradigms to stress-test — programming the molecule and borrowing the immune system, programming the cell and verifying it before release, or programming the cell at runtime, inside the patient.

questions:
  - If evolution already placed macrophages in every solid tumour, were 30 years of ex vivo T-cell engineering solving the wrong delivery problem?
  - Is verification the product, or the bottleneck? Does "verified before release" survive contact with an in vivo therapy you can re-dose, titrate and stop?
  - If you had £100M, would you spend it on better ways to write to the immune system, or better ways to read it back?
  - In 2040, is the deliverable a therapy course or a restored immune state — and would any regulator accept "immune state" as a registrable endpoint?
  - Will the first durable solid-tumour immunotherapy be a smarter molecule, a smarter cell, or a smarter LNP?
